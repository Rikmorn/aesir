Database load: what loads the store and where it saturates

Two things hold before any measurement and the evidence confirms both. A paused conversation costs nothing: load is proportional to active turns and to the count of infrastructure that polls, never to the conversations waiting. And the store is off the token path: a model call takes seconds and a turn issues a handful of statements around it, so the work rate is bounded by active turns divided by model latency.

What moved from the first-principles draft. Transcript reads dropped from second to sixth: a live transcript is bounded by the context window (about 0.7 MB at 200K tokens, 3.5 MB at 1M), so the cost is the harness's decode latency, not the store's I/O. LISTEN/NOTIFY dropped to last. The deployment shape's effect on the store turned out to be connections, not query rate: idle polling at twenty replicas is ten statements a second and does not matter. Two levers are not levers: synchronous_commit = off gains at most 15% on NVMe, and a dedicated queue below thousands of tasks a second buys nothing the precedents needed. Batching is the lever, 10× to 20×.

Deployment shape: one Deployment per agent, or a shared pool

No surveyed product uses one Deployment per agent as its scaling unit except kagent's v0.10 line, and kagent's v1.0.0-alpha releases (18 to 27 September) replace it with a WorkerPool per namespace, a per-session actor that pins a revision, and a snapshot per template revision. Every hosted platform places compute per session; Knative, Cloud Run and Temporal place per version. The per-agent Deployment is a Kubernetes convenience, not an industry pattern.

Per-agent Deployment wins whenThe pool wins when
The agent count is small and stays small (about ten or fewer)The count is fifty or more, or version churn is high: a pinned version costs the pool a registry artefact and costs the per-agent shape seven objects, two queries a minute and a held connection per (agent, version)
Agents need distinct base images or native dependenciesThe paused-to-active ratio is high: every wake of a cold agent pays 20 to 65 s in the per-agent shape and about 1 s in a warm pool
Per-agent identity and egress must be Kubernetes-native for auditThe harness is thin (tens of MiB idle) rather than a 1 GiB subprocess per session

Where the evidence points, for Roberto's decision. The product statement is an organisation of agents, the harness is thin by design, and the workload pauses for days, so three of the pool's conditions hold before a line is written, and the one shipped per-agent precedent abandoned the shape this month. The per-agent shape's remaining argument, Kubernetes-native identity, is answered by a TokenRequest per claimed row plus the gateway the design needs anyway. Proposal: a shared harness pool per trust tier, with a dedicated Deployment only for a bundle that needs a native dependency. If the first workload ran on the Claude Agent SDK as the harness, the 1 GiB per session floor would remove the density argument and the per-session shape would become the comparison.

Speaking to a team of agents

"Swarm" means three things in the field. To OpenAI, LangGraph and Microsoft it is a handoff shape: one conversation moving between agents, one agent holding the human at a time. Anthropic's red team uses it for ten to eighty peers on one project, and documents that shape failing. A fleet is many independent agents on unrelated jobs, which the registry answers. The question here is a team: several agents coordinating on one piece of work. A human can address three things in a team, and every shipped product is a choice among them.

Decided, Roberto, 2026-09-27: delegation depth is one. "I can definitely see the point for single level depth. I find it difficult to think of any use case, coding or not, that really needs more than single depth; if an agent needs to defer as part of a loop, the orchestrator or coordinator can always fill that role." The platform enforces one level; a member that needs more work done asks its coordinator, or the human through the record, and never delegates onward. Depth two leaves the register and the hop-two authorisation gap is moot.

What the team case adds beyond single-agent direct addressing, each with a shipped precedent: a member's question lands in the record under the member's own task id and the lead reads the record rather than relaying it (Linear's per-session elicitation, Symphony's Human Review state; Copilot Studio documents the loss when the parent relays, "a brand-new query"); the team's state is readable without asking the lead (Claude Code's task list, Devin's child session links); the human has a direct line to steer a member (Claude Code, Devin, Managed Agents' user.interrupt). The picture is on the flows page.

What moved from the first-principles draft. The draft had the lead as the default and the record as the first workload. The numbers reverse it for coding: the record is the default and a lead is the gated exception for breadth-first work with a weak single-agent baseline. The draft accepted the proposal's depth two; the vendors' one-level convergence, the drift curve, and Roberto's own reading say one.

Connector tiers: what "free" means for a builder

"Free" decomposes into nine items, and only three of them, the client code, the inbound adapter and rate-limit handling, can be owned by someone other than whoever registered the app with the provider. A GitHub App has one owner account; a Slack app belongs to the workspace that created it and is installed per workspace; a Linear OAuth app gets a distinct app user per workspace. Hosted brokers (Nango, Composio, Arcade, Pipedream, Paragon) register one app for all their customers and still call it a development convenience. A self-hosted platform cannot even offer that. So the operator registers the provider app once per provider per installation, and renews any secret the provider expires. What the platform makes free is the code and the generic machinery: callback URL, consent flow, vault, refresh, egress injection, webhook hosting. GitHub's App Manifest flow returns every secret the vault needs in one call after the user approves; Slack's manifest API does the same; Linear stays manual.

Tier 2 is Tier 1 authored by the platform, which keeps the principle that first-party integrations use the public extension API with no privileged path; Claude runs directory and custom connectors "on the same infrastructure" and Docker strips only the signature from self-provided images. Whether builder code sees a token is not a tier. It is decided by the credential kind: a static token or OAuth access token is substituted at egress, a GitHub App exchange or SigV4 signature needs a platform vending step, and a credential the code must hold is a labelled property of the bundle, allowed but visible. Every mature ecosystem (Home Assistant, Grafana, Terraform, VS Code) keeps that bottom case allowed: forbid it and builders route around the platform with static tokens in images.

The MCP authorization spec (2026-07-28) and where the token lives. Client ID Metadata Documents are the SHOULD, Dynamic Client Registration is deprecated, resource indicators are mandatory, and an MCP server must never pass through the token it received. The spec dissolves the provider-app problem only where the provider itself runs a remote MCP server, and Slack and Linear do. Three placements ship: the MCP client inside the agent process (Claude Code, token on disk), the MCP client at the platform with the token injected when the connection opens (Managed Agents), or the builder's own MCP server as the OAuth client (Cloudflare). Only the second keeps the container credential-free, so the container is never the MCP client.

Inbound events: the five open questions, answered

Every platform that receives webhooks on a builder's behalf (Svix Ingest, Hookdeck, Inngest) owns the URL and records the delivery before any builder code runs. Tool exposure and the model port

Defer-by-default with a pinned core and a search is now a first-party API feature at Anthropic (tool search with defer_loading, public 2025-11-24) and OpenAI (tool_search with namespaces, gpt-5.4 and later), and Claude Code's default once MCP descriptions pass 10% of the window. Measured: 8 to 19 points lost when about three similar tools are added; Anthropic's own 79.5% to 88.1% with search on. Thirty-five tools at 800 tokens each are about 28k tokens a call, 14% of a 200k window; caching removes the money, not the window share. The manifest shape is a pinned core plus a searchable rest, with phase gates as harness policy on top.

The model port options were checked rather than assumed. The Vercel AI SDK exposes nearly the whole Anthropic surface today, including compaction, tool search and mid-conversation changes; its cost is a major version every six to eight months, not lag. LiteLLM keeps the levers only on its passthrough route, needs Postgres and Redis for its features, and shipped a credential stealer on PyPI in March 2026 through a compromised CI action. Option A, an own port, is smaller than assumed because both vendors now share the primitives (opaque round-trip blocks, deferred tools with tail expansion, dated server-tool types) and LiteLLM's 43 supports_* keys are a ready capability vocabulary. Position: A, with a capability declaration per adapter that fails the build when the manifest asks for a lever the provider lacks, and a passthrough proxy as the egress shape only.

Support services and the security inputs

  • Retention. No surveyed vendor deletes a transcript on a timer; only Claude Code's local cache does, and it drew two data-loss reports. Four states on the row: active, archived, deletion-pending (recorded as an event), purged (id, actor, timestamp and hash kept); derived artefacts enumerated on the row so the purge reaches them. The EDPB's February 2026 report finds controllers using masking as deletion; soft-deleted HNSW embeddings are reconstructible.
  • Cost attribution. The OpenTelemetry GenAI conventions have no release and their metrics carry no conversation or user id; Langfuse's Metrics API cannot group by session. Managed Agents is the only system attributing a delegated thread's spend to the caller. Usage stays a domain event per turn in the store, carrying the parent conversation id.
  • Redaction. Every trace backend masks client-side and irreversibly. A typed placeholder block, the original in a tenant-keyed vault, rendered to the model as a sentence, is what Managed Agents' redacted block and Anthropic's signed thinking block already do in different forms.
  • Injection. MCP 2026-07-28 says clients must treat tool annotations as untrusted; tool poisoning, rug pulls and cross-server shadowing are demonstrated; shipped clients refresh on list_changed without a diff. A2A card signatures are only a SHOULD to verify, and a generated card leaked a system prompt in the field. Cards authored and signed, never generated; served curated through the gateway.
  • Sandbox and approvals. Every product runs the loop outside the sandbox. Three credential postures: plaintext (E2B, Modal), a sentinel swapped at a proxy (Managed Agents cloud, Daytona, Claude Code mask), a vended identity (Foundry). Shipped approval gates converge on destructive file operations, egress to new hosts, shell, package installs and PR merge; payments and record deletes are gated by nobody by default; defaults are drifting to classifiers, so any action that needs a human has to be pinned as ask. A fifteen-row threat table is in the research file.

The code survey: what the redesign must not inherit

The runtime bodies the retarget had never read were read, against master at e5b69e1f, every statement with a file:line. All five mechanisms the proposal keeps exist. The implementation has nine defects, ranked by blast radius:

  1. The transcript is one JSONB document per conversation, loaded whole on every claim and rewritten whole at every boundary, the storage shape the store research says to avoid.
  2. The heartbeat fires only after a model response, so any single tool call, sandbox setup or back-off over five minutes is swept and the conversation can be double-claimed.
  3. No interrupt exists: cancel flips the row while the turn keeps running; a tool in flight receives no signal.
  4. A signal to a running conversation is delivered only if the turn ends in a matching wait_for; a completing turn strands its queued signals; a lost-update window exists at the consume.
  5. Nothing sweeps waiting rows: a wait without a timeout waits forever.
  6. SIGTERM never releases claims explicitly.
  7. The group "lock" is a FOR UPDATE outside any transaction.
  8. Phase 2 summarisation is unreachable from the worker; the session projection's tool-artifact extraction never fires (a payload key mismatch).
  9. The worker-loop tests for pause, completion and timeout scheduling are all it.skip; no test covers mid-turn signals, cancellation, ordering or two workers racing one row.

"Keep the mechanism" becomes "keep the mechanism, rewrite the implementation against the G13, G17 and G19 positions, with the deterministic tests first against a fake model adapter". The conversation store is a fresh service with the old schema as a reference, not a starting point.
